    How to play
    1. Everyone antes 5 and gets five dice under a cup. Only you see yours. 1s are wild: a 1 counts as whatever number you need.
    2. Bids count every die at the table, not just yours. A bid is a guess about everyone's dice together: "at least 7 threes" means seven or more dice on the whole table show a 3. Everyone has five dice, so a table of four has 20 in all; the page shows the real number.
    3. On your turn, bid higher, or call Liar! You can bid more dice, or the same number of dice on a higher die. Example: Old Wick says at least 7 threes. You can say 8 threes, or 7 fours, or call Liar.
    4. You can add a chip raise to your bid, up to 25. If someone raised, you owe that much to stay in, and the Bid button pays it. Or fold: you sit the round out and your chips stay in the pot.
    5. No raise can push the pot past 500. When none fits, the table says Pot limit reached.
    6. Calling Liar is free. Then every cup still in answers: keep the cup shut (only your number of those dice goes out, and the house vouches for it) or show and prove (your dice are shown, and you earn an open-hand mark). The table is counted. If the bid holds, the caller lost the call; if it falls, the bidder did. The loser of the call pays. Everyone else still in gets their own chips back, plus an equal share of the loser's chips and of any chips from players who folded. A caller who was right gets a double share. At a two-player table that is the old rule: the winner takes the pot.
    7. Every roll comes from two secrets sealed before anyone looks: yours and the house's. The commitments are on the table from the deal, and the house secret is opened when the round ends. On a tNIGHT table the chips settle on chain, and every round has a numbered public receipt. The counts themselves are the house's word.
