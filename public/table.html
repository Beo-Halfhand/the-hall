        The claim check
        The house checks a called claim against the hand. That’s the house’s word, for now.
        How to play
        1. Everyone antes 5 and gets five cards. Only you see yours.
        2. Pass. Everyone picks one card and slides it to the next seat, all at once: two passes at least, five at most. A pass can carry a raise, 1 to 25. Facing a raise, you pay it to stay in, or fold.
        3. Stop & claim. From the second pass, anyone can stop the passing with a claim, unless a raise is out that pass. Nobody stops by the fifth, and whoever started the round opens.
        4. The claim lap goes round once, from the next seat. On your turn: Claim a poker hand better than the one to beat, true or a bluff; Check to let the claim stand, free; or Call a claim you think is a bluff. Any of those can carry a raise above the highest so far.
        5. A call is checked by the house against the hand. A bluff pays the caller 5 and drops out of the pot; a true claim costs the caller 5. The table only hears true or false.
        6. Stay in or fold. Facing a raise, you pay only the difference to stay in. Fold, and your claim is gone and what you paid stays in the pot. After the lap, anyone short of the highest raise gets one turn to stay in or fold, and can call a claim as they do.
        7. The pot stops at 500 a round. The best standing claim takes two thirds, rounded up; the second best takes the rest; one alone takes the lot. A bluff nobody called still counts. No claims, and the pot waits for the next round.
        8. The deck seal. The deck is shuffled from a seed and sealed before any card moves, and the seal is on the table. When the round ends the seed and the deck open on the round’s receipt, so every hand and every verdict can be rechecked.
